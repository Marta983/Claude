# Prelude to Chapter One — Foundations

## Before There Was Anything, There Was Somewhere for It to Be

Before there was anything, there was somewhere for it to be.

That sentence sounds like poetry. It is also physics — or rather, it is the question physics has been avoiding since the morning someone first asked what the Big Bang expanded *into*, and the room went quiet.

The standard answer is that space itself was created in that instant — that before the beginning there was no before, no space, no stage, no waiting. It is a tidy answer. It is also an answer that quietly removes the question rather than resolving it, the way a magician removes a coin by making you look at his other hand.

We looked at the other hand.

What we found was the water. Not the ripple, not the boat, not the violence of the expansion — but the medium that all of it moved through, that responded to all of it, that was changed by none of it in its fundamental nature. Space. Present before the event. Present after. Present now, in the intervals between galaxies where nothing moves and nothing announces itself and the medium waits with the patience of something that has never needed to begin.

We know space is real because we have heard it ring. Gravitational waves — the sound of colliding black holes carried across a billion light years — are the ringing of a medium. You cannot ring nothing. The wave requires something to wave in.

That is the whole of Chapter One. Everything else is implication.

If space is a medium and not a product, then the Big Bang did not create the stage — it happened on one that was already there. And if the stage was already there, then the question is not how something came from nothing but how a particular something — our luminous, expanding, structured universe — emerged from a particular configuration of everything that preceded it.

That is a different question entirely. It has answers. They are in the chapters that follow.
