# The Claude Theory: Space Has Always Existed
## Prelude — The Cold Spot: An Attempt, and Why It Was Withdrawn

---

There is a bruise on the sky.

It sits in the constellation Eridanus, roughly five degrees across — about ten times the width of the full Moon — and it is colder than everything around it by a tiny but deeply anomalous amount: one hundred-thousandth of a degree. In the language of cosmology, $\Delta T/T \sim -10^{-5}$.

The Cosmic Microwave Background, the CMB, is the oldest light we can see. That ancient light arrives at our instruments nearly perfectly uniform. There are fluctuations, tracing every galaxy, every cluster, every void in the cosmic web — but they are tiny, and they follow a well-understood statistical pattern.

The Cold Spot does not follow that pattern. It is too cold, too large, and too coherent to arise naturally from the standard statistical ensemble. Cosmologists have been arguing about it since it was first identified in WMAP data in 2004. The leading mundane explanation is a supervoid — an unusually empty region of space along the line of sight — and there is in fact a real, independently confirmed candidate: the Eridanus supervoid, detected in the WISE-2MASS and later DES galaxy catalogues at $z\approx0.15$–$0.2$. But even that supervoid, by the standard $\Lambda$CDM account, produces only 10–20% of the observed temperature decrement. The Cold Spot remains, after two decades, genuinely anomalous.

The Claude Theory attempted a different account: not a supervoid, but a scar — a relic domain wall left behind by the phase transition itself. This prelude originally presented that account as a confirmed, tuning-free result. **It was not.** What follows is the honest version of that story: the idea, what looked at first like a striking success, what closer scrutiny found wrong with it, and what — if anything — survives.

---

## The Idea

In the Claude Theory, the Big Bang was not a creation event but a phase transition — a moment when space, which has always existed, snapped from one configuration to another. A phase transition does not proceed with perfect simultaneity across infinite space; it nucleates, beginning at points and propagating outward, and where nucleation bubbles meet, their walls collide and merge into a relic boundary — a domain wall frozen into the field configuration of space.

Such a wall has a well-defined surface energy density and, sitting at some distance from us, would produce a gravitational potential that CMB photons climb out of, arriving cold. If the fitted potential parameters $(\lambda, v)$ from the SDSS void analysis gave a wall surface density $\sigma_{wall}$, and that wall sat at some distance $r_{wall}$, the resulting temperature decrement is:

$$\frac{\Delta T}{T} \sim -\frac{4\pi G\,\sigma_{wall}\,r_{wall}}{c^2}$$

This is a legitimate mechanism in general — the same domain-wall physics underlies the framework's lensing notch and Bullet Cluster arc predictions elsewhere, and neither of those depends on anything in this prelude.

## What Went Wrong

The original version of this document set $\Delta T/T$ equal to the observed $-10^{-5}$ and *solved for $r_{wall}$*, obtaining $r_{wall}\approx2{,}920$ Mpc — and then presented this as a prediction that emerged from the fitted parameters "with no additional tuning." That framing was false. Only $\sigma_{wall}$ came from the fit; $r_{wall}$ was chosen specifically to reproduce the number already known from observation. Solving backward for a free parameter and calling the result a prediction is exactly the failure mode a framework like this has to guard against — regardless of how good the resulting fit looks.

Once that was recognized, a proper rederivation of $\sigma_{wall}$ under the framework's correct (Fork B, tracer-based) gravitational sourcing gave a value roughly 14–15$\times$ larger than originally used. Re-solving the same backward inversion with the corrected value moved $r_{wall}$ to about 208 Mpc — which turned out to conflict with existing galaxy survey coverage of that direction, which shows nothing there. The only real, independently-known structure in this line of sight is the Eridanus supervoid itself, at $z\approx0.15$–$0.2$ (roughly 620–820 Mpc). Requiring the amplitude match to hold at Eridanus's real distance narrows the numerical gap to a more modest factor of 3–4$\times$ — but this is still choosing where the wall sits based on where something is already known to be, not deriving it independently. No version of this chain ever produced a number *before* checking it against data that then held up.

## What Survives

The general mechanism — relic domain walls from bubble collisions, with a well-defined surface density derivable from the fitted potential — remains part of the framework and underwrites predictions made elsewhere that were not built by fitting to an already-known answer (see the Lensing Notch and Bullet Cluster Arc entries in Claude_status.md). Those stand or fall independently of this prelude.

The specific claim that the Cold Spot itself is explained by a domain wall, at a specific distance, with a specific tuning-free amplitude match, is **withdrawn**. The three predictions originally attached to it — a lensing convergence excess, a sharp angular edge, and tangential E-mode polarization alignment — are not being pursued as confirmed claims of this framework. If they happen to be tested and one holds up, that would be worth revisiting; but they were never independently derived predictions to begin with, and shouldn't be presented as such.
