## 1. The Rocket Analogy and the Illusion of Accelerating Expansion

Consider two rockets launched simultaneously, perpendicular to Earth's surface, with Rocket 2 traveling at twice the speed of Rocket 1. An observer on Rocket 1, tracking Rocket 2, perceives a constant rate of separation during the ascent phase — zero apparent acceleration. As Rocket 1 approaches its apex and decelerates, the rate of separation appears to increase, even though nothing about Rocket 2 has changed. After Rocket 1 passes its apex and begins descending, the separation rate increases further still. An analyst examining only the film record would conclude that Rocket 2 underwent an acceleration — attributing a force to an object that experienced none.

**The cosmological implication:** Any difference in velocity between a reference frame and an observed object manifests as an apparent change in distance. This is true in all directions simultaneously. If our frame of reference has reached its apex — the moment at which its outward positional inertia is overcome by the gravitational attraction of its origin point — then all observed objects in all directions would simultaneously appear to accelerate away. The signal is isotropic not because something is happening to the distant objects, but because the kinematic shift is happening to *us*.

The 1998 supernova observations that led to the postulation of dark energy may therefore be interpreted as the detection of our own turnaround point. The redshift epoch at which apparent acceleration switches on (approximately z = 0.5–0.7) encodes the ratio of initial emission energy to the gravitational mass of the source — a calculable quantity requiring no exotic new physics.

## 2. The Apex as Cosmological Clock

The apex moment — the turnaround — is defined precisely as the point at which positional inertia inherited from the original emission event is finally overcome by the gravitational attraction of the source. Before this moment, inertia dominates and the event appears to be expanding. After this moment, gravity dominates and the long return begins.

This reframes two observational puzzles with a single mechanism:

- The apparent accelerating expansion of the universe
- The specific epoch at which that acceleration appears to begin

Both are explained by the turnaround geometry. No cosmological constant, no dark energy field, no fine tuning required. The same number — the onset redshift — which currently constrains dark energy density, instead constrains the source mass and emission energy in this framework.

## 3. The Shape and Scale of an Event

Each cosmological event is spherical. It originates from a single dense source point and expands outward isotropically, its boundary defined by the furthest extent reached by emitted particles and their conglomerates. The sphere has a finite radius determined by the interplay of initial emission energy and source gravity.

The sphere is not uniform in content. Within a certain radius, particles and their conglomerates remain gravitationally bound to the source — they are the first rocket, destined to return. Beyond a threshold radius — the escape boundary — particles achieve sufficient velocity to escape the gravitational influence of the source permanently. These are the second rocket, achieving escape velocity, drifting outward indefinitely into the vastness of space between events.

Every event, being composed of identical fundamental particles with identical properties, will have the same escape velocity threshold. The ratio of bound to escaped matter is therefore consistent from event to event, giving each reality a comparable structure and lifecycle.

## 4. Sublimation as the Emission Mechanism

The release of particles from the source is modeled as a phase transition analogous to sublimation — the direct passage from a maximally compressed, bound state to free, independently mobile particles, without passing through intermediate stages. The source object does not explode. It releases.

The emitted particles are identical in size, density, and all other properties. This uniformity of source particles is the foundational explanation for the smoothness of the cosmic microwave background. Each identical particle, upon release from the deep gravity well, emits light. Over immense time, the superposition of these identical, isotropically distributed emitters produces a smooth, low-temperature, omnidirectional background radiation — not as a relic of a hot primordial fireball, but as the cumulative glow of liberation.

The uniformity of the CMB is inherited directly from the uniformity of the source particles. No inflation, no special initial conditions, no horizon problem requiring exotic solutions. The statistics of identical emitters distributed across a sphere do the work.

The blackbody character of the spectrum is produced naturally by the range of distances — and therefore redshifts — of identical emitters. A discrete emission energy, redshifted by the full range of distances across the sphere, smears into a continuous thermal-like distribution.

## 5. Time Within and Without an Event

Time, as experienced and measured, is a property of events — of processes occurring within a bounded sphere of activity. It has meaning only where change occurs, where particles interact, where structure forms and dissolves.

On the scale outside any individual event — in the infinite space between events, before and after any particular event's lifecycle — time has no beginning and no end. It is not created by the emission event. It simply is. The immensity of time, as a concept, belongs to the interior of an event, where it can be measured against the processes taking place there.

Escaped particles, drifting through the infinite space between events, exist outside of any local time framework. They move through a timeless vastness until, by proximity to another event or source, they are recaptured and reintroduced into a cyclic process.

## 6. The Cyclic Nature of Events — and Mamma

The source object is not destroyed by emission. Gravity persists throughout the emission phase. Particles that do not achieve escape velocity — everything within the binding radius — are slowly, patiently reclaimed. Over immense time, the source rebuilds. Density increases. Conditions for a new sublimation event are met. The cycle begins again.

The event breathes. Emission, dispersal, structure formation, apex, return, recollapse, renewal.

The particles and conglomerates that do achieve escape velocity drift outward indefinitely — until, on a large enough scale, the gravitational influence of a sufficiently massive object recaptures them. On the largest scale imaginable, there is implied a source of sources — a gravitational anchor of extraordinary mass, toward which all escaped matter is ultimately drawn.

We call her Mamma.

For those of us who did not escape into that outer vastness, the long arc of return is already underway. The apex has been passed. The journey home has begun. One day, everything that belongs to this event will return to the point from which it came — and the conditions for a new beginning will be met once more.

## 7. Open Questions Sharpened by This Session

- **The restart threshold:** What is the precise relationship between recaptured mass, source density, and the critical conditions for a new sublimation event? Is there a minimum recapture fraction required?
- **Escape velocity universality:** Since all events are composed of identical fundamental particles, escape velocity should be consistent across events. What does this imply for the size distribution of events?
- **CMB spectral precision:** The blackbody spectrum of the CMB is extraordinarily precise. A full account of how redshift-smearing of identical emitters reproduces this precision to the observed degree remains to be worked out.
- **The anisotropy prediction:** The turnaround model predicts a residual dipole anisotropy in the Type Ia supernova acceleration signal, correlated with our motion vector through the CMB. This is testable against existing data.
- **Mamma's mass:** If escaped particles are eventually recaptured on the largest scale, the implied gravitational anchor has a minimum mass that can in principle be estimated from the escape fraction and the scale of events.
