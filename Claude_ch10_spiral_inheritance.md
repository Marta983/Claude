# The Claude Theory: Space Has Always Existed
## Chapter 10 — Spiral Inheritance and Electromagnetic Geometry

---

## The Observational Foundation

At every scale where resolution permits, collapsing or ejecting mass organizes into planar or axial geometry rather than spherical geometry. Galaxies are predominantly flat disks. Stellar death events — supernovae, neutron star mergers — produce bipolar outflows, toroidal ejecta, and jet structures, not spherical shells. Planetary systems are flat. The cosmic web itself, while not flat in a simple sense, is concentrated in sheets and filaments bounding void regions rather than distributed uniformly through space.

The preponderance of evidence does not support a universe whose default geometry is spherical. The burden of proof runs the other way.

---

## The Perpendicular Force and Its Consequence

A moving charged object experiences a force acting perpendicular to its direction of motion. This is foundational electrodynamics — the same physics that occupied Einstein and his contemporaries in the analysis of moving bodies, and whose geometric implications led directly to special relativity.

The field of a moving charge does not broadcast spherically. It flattens into a disk perpendicular to the direction of motion. The faster the charge moves, the more compressed this disk becomes. Flattening is not incidental — it is a geometric consequence of motion itself at the electromagnetic level.

A charged object subject to a perpendicular force does not travel in a straight line. It curves. And if it is losing energy — as all real charged particles do through radiation — the curve tightens into a spiral. The spiral is the natural path of a charged, radiating object moving through a magnetic field.

This is observed directly: cosmic ray particles spiral along magnetic field lines; electrons in synchrotron sources trace spiral paths; charged particles in bubble chambers leave the characteristic inward spiral of energy loss. The spiral is not exotic. It is the default.

## Spiral Geometry Inherited Through Casting

Rotating, magnetized stellar objects — stars, neutron stars, black holes — eject material when they die. That material carries charge. It moves outward through the magnetic field of the central object. The perpendicular electromagnetic force acts on it immediately.

The ejecta therefore do not fly outward radially like spokes from a wheel. They follow spiral paths, winding outward along the field lines of the progenitor object. The geometry of what gets deposited in the surrounding medium is not spherical, not even simply disk-like — it is spiral, with angular momentum and electromagnetic orientation already encoded in the distribution.

Within the Claude Theory's casting mechanics, this has a direct consequence: the seeds deposited by tidal casting are not isotropically distributed. They arrive along spiral paths. The receiving medium inherits a spiral geometry from the electromagnetic trajectory of the arriving material, not merely from the gravitational influence of the progenitor.

## Galaxies as Fossil Records

The spiral arms of a galaxy may not require special explanation as a dynamical feature of the disk. They may be the fossil record of how the material arrived.

If the matter that built a galaxy was delivered along outward spiral electromagnetic paths from a rotating, magnetized progenitor — a dying star, a neutron star, a black hole aggregate — then the spiral structure of the resulting galaxy reflects the geometry of those delivery paths. The arms are not created by density waves propagating through a pre-existing disk. They are the imprint of the trajectories along which the disk's material was deposited.

This is consistent with the observation that spiral arm pitch angle varies among galaxies — different progenitor field strengths and rotation rates would produce different spiral geometries in the deposited material.

## The Eruption Event as Anisotropic

The accumulated pre-eruption mass — the dark remnant population accumulated over vast timescales — consists of rotating, magnetized objects. The aggregate has net angular momentum. It has net electromagnetic structure. When the bifurcation threshold derived in Chapter 7 is reached and the eruption fires, this angular momentum and electromagnetic geometry are not erased. They are inherited by the eruption itself.

The eruption event is therefore temporally simultaneous — the threshold fires everywhere in the accumulated field at once — but spatially anisotropic. The expansion has a preferred plane, determined by the net angular momentum of the accumulated remnant population.

Everything downstream of the eruption inherits this preferred plane: the large-scale structure of the resulting observable universe, the orientation of galaxy formation, the geometry of void distribution. The CMB uniformity is preserved — the transition fired simultaneously — but the CMB itself may carry a faint geometric imprint of the eruption's preferred orientation, potentially manifesting as the large-scale anomalies observed in the CMB power spectrum.

## Testable Implications

Several observational predictions follow from the spiral inheritance framework:

**Pitch angle vs. progenitor properties.** If spiral arm geometry is inherited from progenitor field structure, a systematic relationship should exist between arm pitch angle and the properties of the galactic center's dark remnant population — its mass, rotation rate, and magnetic field strength.

**Orientation correlation at large scale.** If the eruption event had a preferred plane, the large-scale distribution of voids and filaments should show a statistically detectable orientation preference. This connects to the observed anomalous alignments in quasar polarization vectors across cosmological distances, and to the CMB "axis of evil" — large-scale power asymmetries that have resisted explanation within the standard isotropic model.

**Ejecta geometry as seed geometry.** The geometry of stellar death ejecta — observable directly in nearby supernovae and neutron star mergers — should correlate with the geometry of subsequent structure formation in the same region, if casting mechanics are operative.
