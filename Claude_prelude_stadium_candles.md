# The Stadium, the Candles, and the Uniformly Faint Glow

Imagine you are leaving a stadium. Not in a panic — everyone is calm, purposeful. You take the hands of the people on either side of you, and the people behind you do the same, and the people behind them, ring after ring, all the way back to the last row.

You all walk out together. Each person walks a straight path — but since you all came from the same center point, your straight paths diverge very slightly from your neighbors'. Seen from above, what looked like rows becomes rings. Rings that grow larger and larger as you walk further from the gate.

At some point your arms are no longer long enough. You let go. But you don't lose your place — you're still the same distance from the origin as your fellows. The ring holds its identity even without the hands.

Now give everyone a candle.

Each candle burns with the same steady flame. But as the rings grow larger, those flames are spread over an ever greater circumference. An observer standing at the origin sees light arriving from all directions — uniformly — but growing fainter and fainter as the rings expand.

Not because the candles are dimming.

Because the rings are growing.

---

## The Herald

There is something else to notice about those candles.

The light from each one — being massless, being a disturbance in the medium of space itself rather than a thing with weight and substance — does not travel with its bearer. It races ahead. It diverges from the path of the person carrying it, spreading in all directions from the moment it is born, at the only speed the medium permits.

The candle bearer curves with gravity. The light does not. It has no handle for those forces to grab.

So what reaches the observer at the origin is not the walkers.

It is the glow of all their candles, arriving from every direction equally, carried by heralds who left their bearers behind almost immediately and have been traveling in straight lines ever since.

You are sitting inside that glow right now.

It has a name. We call it the Cosmic Microwave Background.

It is not a mystery. It is what candles do.

## The Flattening

Close to the origin the rings are tight. The curve is steep. The geometry is nearly a sphere.

But as the rings expand, something changes. The circumference grows faster than the eye can follow. What was a gentle curve becomes, at sufficient distance, practically a plane. The curvature becomes imperceptible at local scales.

A surveyor on a vast enough plain does not need to know the Earth is round. It looks flat because the curve is so gradual at that scale.

And every measure you'd care to apply — temperature, intensity, the glow of candles per unit of arc — becomes uniform across that flattening surface. Not because of a fine-tuning miracle. Not because of a special mechanism applied in the first fraction of a second. But because you are sampling an ever larger section of an ever more gently curving ring.

The famous flatness problem — why is the universe so geometrically flat? — and the horizon problem — why is the CMB so exquisitely uniform in all directions? — have troubled cosmology for decades. Inflation was invented largely to answer them.

But they are not problems here. They are consequences. Geometric inevitabilities baked into the structure from the first moment of departure.

It could not have been otherwise.

---

## The Toroid

Now notice what happens at the back of the crowd.

The source stops. The last stragglers leave the entrance. And gravity — which has been working on every ring from the beginning, more and more powerfully on those with less momentum, less distance, less time to escape — begins to tell.

The early rings are too far gone. They continue outward, candles growing fainter, arms long since released, too distant for gravity to recall.

But the stragglers slow. They curve. They do not fall straight back to the center — they carry lateral momentum from their original departure angle. So they sweep around it. They become the inner surface of a toroid, a great ring of returning walkers joining others returning from slightly different angles, forming a structure that is neither escaped nor collapsed but perpetually curving around what was.

Seen from above — from outside — you have an expanding outer frontier of rings still moving outward, and within it a toroidal structure of those recalled, sweeping around a center that is no longer emitting but is not empty.

Voids. Filaments. The great web of cosmic structure.

Not imposed from outside. Not requiring special initial conditions.

Just what happens when a stadium empties, everyone carries a candle, and gravity is patient.
