# Rings, Ratios, and the Recoverable Center

## The Triangle and the Hidden Geometry

Begin with a simple triangle. Two sides are radii — equal in length, anchored at a common center, spreading outward at a fixed angle between them. The third side is the chord connecting their outer endpoints.

The chord length is:

$$c = 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$

Where r is the radius and θ is the fixed angle between them. As r increases, c increases proportionally. The ratio between them never changes. The triangle grows but its proportions are frozen — baked in at the moment the angle was established.

At θ = 60°, something precise happens. The chord equals the radius. All three sides become equal. An equilateral triangle, which remains equilateral no matter how large r grows.

Now suppose you are an observer who does not know which sides are radii and which is the chord. You measure three distances. You find them scaling uniformly, ratios preserved, everything internally consistent.

You would have no way of knowing that two of those sides are arms of a rotating expanding structure anchored to an origin you cannot see. You might conclude you are looking at three equivalent independent distances in flat space.

Your conclusions, built carefully on that assumption, would be missing something fundamental.

Not through negligence. Not through error. Simply through the irreducible limitation of measuring from within.

---

## What Changing Ratios Tell You

Now suppose the ratios are not maintained. The chord grows faster or slower than the radii predict.

There is only one explanation: the angle between the radii is changing.

And a changing angle means the origin point is not a fixed silent anchor. It is active. Still participating. Pushing the arms apart or drawing them together.

An embedded observer who does not know this — who sees only the changing distances — might conclude that space itself is accelerating. That some mysterious force is required to account for what is seen. That a new term is needed in the equations.

When what may actually be observed is a changing angle. A geometry that is not simply scaling but opening.

The cosmological constant. Dark energy. These may be the names we have given to a changing θ.

## Sorting the Rings

This is where the geometry becomes a tool.

If ratio maintenance identifies two points on the same ring — same distance from origin, same moment of departure, same radius — then ratio change identifies points on different rings.

The direction of the change tells you which way:

Chord growing faster than expected — the points are diverging, on different rings, one further from the origin than the other.

Chord growing slower — the points may be on a ring being recalled by gravity, part of the structure forming behind the expansion frontier.

So from pure distance measurements — no other information required — the ring structure becomes recoverable. Group the constant-ratio pairs onto the same ring. Use the changing ratios to establish the spacing between rings. Build the geometry from the inside out.

The observations already being collected contain this information. The measurements already exist. They have not yet been sorted by this criterion.

## The Center

If the rings can be identified, can anything be said about a center they may share?

Yes. In principle, everything.

Each identified ring is a circle. A circle implies a center. Two concentric circles constrain that center considerably. Three rings constrain it further. With enough rings, all sharing the same origin, the center can be triangulated with increasing precision.

We are embedded observers. We are on one of the rings ourselves. We cannot look down from above and find the center directly. We must infer it from the pattern of what surrounds us.

But the ring structure carries the information. The ratios, the spacing between rings, the way brightness falls off with distance — all of it points back toward an origin.

Not with a sign saying *here*.

But with a geometry that has only one solution.

The center is not unknowable.

It is simply not yet looked for in quite this way.
