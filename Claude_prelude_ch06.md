# Prelude to Chapter Six — The SDSS Void Fit

## Three Numbers

There is a moment in the life of any theory when it must stop talking and start calculating. When the elegant idea, the careful argument, the poetic framing must make way for a different kind of honesty: here are numbers, here is data, here is whether they agree.

Chapter Six is that moment.

The three parameters that govern the vacancy field — $\epsilon_0$, $\lambda$, and $v$ — have until now been placeholders. Named, defined, given roles to play in the equations, but not pinned to actual values. A theory with unnamed parameters is not wrong, but it is not yet tested. It is a key that has not been tried in any lock.

The lock is the SDSS void catalog. The Sloan Digital Sky Survey mapped hundreds of thousands of galaxies across a significant fraction of the observable sky, and from that map researchers extracted the statistical properties of the cosmic voids — their sizes, their density profiles, the shape of the boundary between nearly-empty interior and galaxy-rich wall. That profile has been published. It has numbers attached to it. It is, in the language of science, a constraint.

---

The vacancy field predicts a specific void profile shape — the same hyperbolic tangent that describes the domain wall's transition between its two stable states. That shape either fits the observed profile or it does not. There is no adjusting the shape after the fact; the mathematics determines it.

It fits. Not perfectly — no model of the real universe fits perfectly — but with a coefficient of determination of 0.915, meaning the domain wall profile accounts for more than nine tenths of the variation in the observed void density distribution. For a profile derived from first principles, from a potential whose form was determined by theoretical requirements rather than by fitting to this data, that is a result worth pausing on.

From that fit, three numbers emerge with error bars attached: $v = 0.327$, $\lambda = 0.1175$, $\epsilon_0 = 1.73 \times 10^{-3}$. They are not chosen. They are found.

---

And then something important happens. Those three numbers, fixed by the void profile fit, are carried forward into calculations they were not fitted to.

The lensing form factor — the profile of gravitational deflection around a domain wall — is now not a free prediction but a constrained one. The wall thickness is $6.31$ megaparsecs per unit of the Hubble parameter. The lensing suppression at the wall center, the recovery to full deflection at the edges, the spatial scale over which the transition happens — all of it is now a number, not a shape. And that number can be compared to weak lensing surveys that already exist.

The redshift evolution of void sizes — the prediction that characteristic void radius scales as $(1+z)^2$, growing larger at lower redshift as the universe expands and the asymmetry deepens — can be checked against the two redshift bins in the BOSS DR16 catalog. The prediction gives a growth ratio of 1.49 between those bins. The observed ratio is 1.41. The agreement is within five percent. From two data points, that is not confirmation — but it is not contradiction either, and the prediction lands in the right place.

The CMB Cold Spot — a region of the cosmic microwave background that is anomalously cold, debated for decades, still without a fully satisfying explanation — can be estimated from the fitted void profile. The calculation gives a temperature decrement with the correct sign and within a factor of four of the observed magnitude, derived without invoking an unusually large supervoid or any additional parameters.

---

There is an old distinction in science between fitting and predicting. Fitting is easy: given enough parameters, any curve can be made to pass through any data. Predicting is hard: use your parameters, fixed by one measurement, to say something checkable about a different measurement before you look at it.

Chapter Six does both. It fits the void profile and finds three numbers. It then predicts, from those numbers alone, the lensing scale, the redshift evolution, and the cold spot temperature. Each of those predictions is either consistent with existing data or waiting for a test that has not yet been done.

That waiting is not weakness. It is the theory doing what theories are supposed to do: making itself vulnerable to being wrong, in specific ways, in specific places, with specific numbers attached.

The key has been cut. The locks are being tried.
